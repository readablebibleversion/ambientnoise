To sleep on a plane, block out the cabin with a steady low sound like brown noise through good headphones, use an eye mask and neck support, and time your sleep to match your destination. Brown noise works especially well because its deep rumble blends with engine drone and hides sudden sounds like chimes, trolleys and conversations. Add a comfortable seat choice, layers and a simple wind-down routine and you give yourself a real chance at rest.

## Why it is so hard to sleep on a plane

Planes are not designed for sleep. You are sitting upright, the cabin is dry, the lights come and go, and there is a constant stream of interruptions: announcements, meal service, people climbing past and the occasional bump of turbulence.

Noise is a big part of the problem. The engines produce a constant roar, but that is actually the easier part to live with. It is the *sudden* sounds on top of the roar, like chimes, crying babies and clattering carts, that tend to pull you out of light sleep.

## Brown noise vs engine drone

Engine drone is loud and steady, with a lot of low-frequency energy. You cannot switch it off, but you can work with it.

- **Brown noise** has most of its energy in the low frequencies, so it sits right on top of the engine drone. Instead of fighting it, brown noise blends with it, turning the cabin into one even, deep sound. Sudden noises then stand out less.
- **Pink noise** is a little brighter and helps cover the middle range, where voices and announcements live. A small amount mixed in can help.
- **White noise** is very hissy and often feels harsh at plane volumes, so most people prefer brown or pink.

Adding soft ocean waves on top gives the mix a more natural, restful feel. For more on the differences, see our guide to [white, pink and brown noise](/blog/white-pink-brown-noise/).

## Headphones and volume

The right headphones make sleep on a plane much easier.

- **Noise-cancelling headphones** reduce the engine roar, so you can play your sleep sound at a lower volume. Over-ear models are often the most effective but can be bulky when leaning against a window.
- **Low-profile earbuds** are more comfortable if you sleep on your side or lean your head on a pillow.
- **Earplugs** are a simple, cheap backup, and some people wear them under over-ear headphones.

Keep the volume moderate. It is tempting to turn things up over the engine noise, but long listening at high volume is not kind to your ears. Our guide on how loud white noise should be has more detail. A sleep timer, or a mix that fades after a while, can help you avoid listening at volume all night. See [sleep sounds all night or on a timer](/blog/sleep-sounds-all-night-or-timer/) for the pros and cons.

Inflight internet is often unreliable, so prepare a backup, such as a downloaded track, before you board.

## Time your sleep around your destination

When you sleep on a flight matters as much as how.

- **Overnight flights:** try to sleep as soon as is practical, often after the meal service. Some people eat before boarding so they can rest straight away.
- **Daytime flights heading into evening:** a short nap may be fine, but too much sleep can make it hard to sleep on arrival.
- **Long-haul with big time changes:** set your watch to the destination time when you board and try to sleep when it would be night there.

Caffeine late in the day can make it harder to fall asleep, and alcohol can make sleep lighter and more broken, as well as adding to dehydration. If you are thinking about sleep aids or medication, talk to a doctor before your trip rather than experimenting on the plane.

## Comfort: seat, support and layers

Small comfort choices add up.

- **Pick a window seat** if you want something to lean against and fewer people climbing past you.
- **Use good neck support.** A travel pillow that stops your head from dropping forward can make a big difference.
- **Wear an eye mask.** Cabin lights, screens and window shades all change through the flight.
- **Dress in layers.** Cabins can swing from warm to chilly. A soft hoodie or scarf doubles as a blanket.
- **Loosen up.** Comfortable clothes, loosened shoes and compression socks, if recommended for you, can help on long flights.
- **Stay hydrated**, and get up to stretch and walk when it is safe to do so.

Let the crew know if you do not want to be woken for meals. Many airlines have a way to signal this.

## A simple in-flight wind-down

Your body likes routines, even in the air. Try a short version of your usual bedtime habits:

1. **Put on comfortable layers** and get your pillow and mask ready.
2. **Start your sleep mix** at a low volume.
3. **Dim your screen** or switch it off.
4. **Take a few slow breaths**, with a longer exhale than inhale.
5. **Let go of forcing it.** Even resting with your eyes closed is better than nothing.

## Tips for nervous flyers who want to sleep

If you are nervous about flying, sleeping can feel impossible, especially during takeoff or turbulence. Be gentle with yourself.

- **Start calming sounds before takeoff** so they are already familiar when the engines spool up.
- **Focus on rest, not sleep.** Closing your eyes and breathing slowly to a steady sound is still restorative.
- **Use extra support.** [SkyCalm](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skycalm-fear-of-flying/id6746972686) is an iPhone app for people who are nervous about flying. Our guide to [sounds for fear of flying](/blog/sounds-for-fear-of-flying/) covers more coping ideas.

If fear of flying is severe or causes panic attacks, talk to a doctor or mental health professional. Help is available.

## The short version

To sleep on a plane, use brown noise to blend with engine drone, wear noise-cancelling headphones or earbuds at a moderate volume, and add an eye mask and neck pillow. Time your sleep around your destination, avoid late caffeine and alcohol, and keep a simple wind-down routine. If nerves are what keep you awake, start calming sounds before takeoff and consider [SkyCalm](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skycalm-fear-of-flying/id6746972686), an iPhone app for people who are nervous about flying.
