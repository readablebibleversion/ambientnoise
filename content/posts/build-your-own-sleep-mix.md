To build your own sleep mix, start with a steady base noise such as pink or brown noise, add one nature sound on top, balance the volumes so nothing jumps out, and set a sleep timer if you do not want sound all night. Save the result as a share link so you can open the same mix every night. Two or three well-balanced layers usually beat a crowded mix.

## Why a custom mix works better than a single sound

Single sounds have trade-offs. White noise masks well but can feel harsh. Rain is cozy but can have uneven moments. Ocean waves are soothing but rise and fall, so quiet troughs can let outside noise through.

Layering lets you combine the strengths. A noise layer keeps the background level steady and covers gaps, while a nature layer gives your mind something pleasant and familiar to rest on. You also get to tune the mix to your room, your ears, and the noises that tend to wake you.

## Step 1: Choose your base noise layer

The base layer is your foundation. Its job is to be steady and fill the space.

- **Pink noise** is a balanced, soft "shhh." It is a great default for most bedrooms. Learn more in [what is pink noise](/blog/what-is-pink-noise/).
- **Brown noise** is deeper, like a distant waterfall or a low rumble. It is helpful when the problem is traffic, footsteps, or a partner's snoring. It can feel especially cozy on cold nights.
- **White noise** is bright and hissy. It masks high-pitched sounds well but some people find it sharp. Use it at a lower level or blend it with pink or brown.

Not sure? Start with pink. If outside rumbles still bother you, switch to brown.

## Step 2: Add a nature layer

Next, add one main nature sound that you find calming. This is the "character" of your mix.

- **Rain** is the most popular sleep sound for good reason: familiar, steady, and cozy.
- **Ocean waves** suit people who like a slow rhythm.
- **A stream** adds gentle movement without big peaks.
- **Fire** feels warm, though the pops can be eventful.
- **Wind** adds space and works well with almost anything.

Pick the one that feels safest and most pleasant to you. If you want something specific, like rain on a tin roof, you can use the [archive search](/#archive) to find a real recording on the Internet Archive and add it to your mix.

## Step 3: Add an optional detail layer

A third layer, kept quiet, can make a mix feel more alive. Good details include:

- **Night crickets** for a summer evening feel.
- **Distant thunder** for a cozy storm (keep it low so it does not startle).
- **Birds at dusk** for a gentle wind-down, then let a timer fade them out.

Keep this layer clearly softer than the others. If you find yourself listening to it rather than drifting off, lower it or remove it.

## Step 4: Balance the volumes

This is where a good mix becomes a great one. A simple method:

1. **Set the base noise first.** Turn it up until it starts to soften the sounds around you, then stop.
2. **Bring in the nature layer.** Raise it until it sits on top of the noise, clearly audible but not dominating.
3. **Add the detail last.** Keep it just at the edge of hearing.
4. **Check the overall level.** Lie down in your sleeping position and listen for a minute. The whole mix should be comfortable, ideally no louder than a quiet conversation. Lower is usually better for long listening.
5. **Test with real noise.** If you are mixing to cover a specific sound, like a snoring partner or a noisy street, test it when that noise is actually happening and adjust.

A balanced starting point might be pink noise around the middle, rain slightly lower, and crickets low. You can [try that mix here](/#mix=pink:0.5,rain:0.4,night:0.2) and nudge the sliders from there.

## Step 5: Set a sleep timer, or do not

Some people want sound all night, others prefer it to fade after they fall asleep.

- **All night** makes sense if noise tends to wake you later, for example early-morning traffic, a partner who gets up early, or a snorer.
- **A timer** of 30 to 90 minutes suits people who mainly need help falling asleep and prefer a quieter room afterward.

If you are unsure, try a timer for a week, then all night for a week, and see which leaves you feeling better. There is no single right answer.

## Step 6: Save it as a share link

Once you like your mix, save it. On AmbientNoise.Online, your mix can be stored as a link that remembers every sound and its volume.

- **Bookmark it** on your phone or laptop so it is one tap away at bedtime.
- **Make a few versions**, such as a "noisy night" mix with more brown noise and a "quiet night" mix with less.
- **Share it** with a partner, friend, or family member who sleeps in the same space.

Using the same mix each night also helps it become a bedtime cue, part of the routine that tells your brain it is time to wind down.

## Troubleshooting your mix

- **"I can still hear the street."** Switch the base to brown noise or raise it slightly.
- **"It feels harsh."** Lower or remove any white noise, and reduce the overall volume.
- **"I keep listening to it."** Remove the detail layer or choose a steadier nature sound.
- **"I wake up when it stops."** Use a longer timer or let it play all night.
- **"Nothing helps."** If you regularly struggle to sleep, talk to a doctor. Persistent sleep problems are common and often treatable.
